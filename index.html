Key findings

Across all 100 NC counties, electric bills and household income vary sharply, and both line up with county racial composition.

The pattern holds up two ways. Counties with a larger Black population share tend to have higher average electric bills, and when bills are measured against local income instead of in raw dollars, the relationship gets even stronger. The same direction shows up in gas bills too, which points toward a shared underlying driver, likely income, housing age, and rural utility pricing, rather than something specific to how electric rates are set.

Data & methods

Three public datasets, merged at the county level.

EnergyU.S. DOE LEAD Tool, 2022 release — household-weighted estimates of monthly electricity and gas cost, aggregated to the county level from raw weighted microdata using each county's total housing-unit weight.

IncomeU.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 5-year estimates (2015–2019 vintage) — median household income per county.

DemographicsNCDHHS, Race and Ethnicity by County, July 1, 2022 postcensal population estimates.

Electric-bill-as-%-of-income is calculated as (average monthly electric bill × 12) ÷ median household income. Income is a median, not an average, so it isn't pulled upward by a county's highest earners the way a mean would be. All other figures are county averages, not individual household bills.

Limitations

This is county-level, correlational data, not household-level or causal.
  • Ecological correlation. A pattern across counties doesn't prove anything about individual households within them. Two counties can have the same % Black population and very different internal distributions of who pays what.
  • Confounding by rurality. Rural NC counties tend to have both higher electric rates (often served by cooperatives rather than Duke Energy) and, in parts of the state, higher Black population share. County size correlates with several of these variables and hasn't been fully controlled for here.
  • No individual billing data. None of the sources contain actual utility bills; they're modeled estimates from survey and administrative data.
  • Different reference years. Electric/gas bills are 2022 LEAD estimates; demographic figures are July 2022 postcensal estimates; median household income is from the Census Bureau's 2015–2019 ACS 5-year estimates, the most recent county-complete vintage available here. Incomes have risen since then, so the burden percentages are a reasonable but not exact read on today's households.
